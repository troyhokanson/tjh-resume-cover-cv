# Cover Letter

August 3, 2026

Hiring Team  
National General, an Allstate company

Dear Hiring Team,

The Claims Adjuster Trainee role makes sense for the next stage of my career because it combines two kinds of work I have done for years: determining what happened after a vehicle incident and helping people through an unpleasant day. I am deliberately pursuing an insurance trainee role because I want to learn coverage, liability, damage evaluation, and claim handling from the ground up. I am not expecting my prior title to substitute for that training.

Traffic safety became personal to me after my grandmother and aunt were killed in a rural South Dakota crash. During 25 years with the Lakeville Police Department, I investigated traffic crashes throughout my patrol assignments. I gathered driver and witness accounts, compared conflicting explanations, photographed vehicles and scenes, documented roadway and environmental conditions, reviewed available records, preserved evidence, and wrote reports that had to withstand later scrutiny. As a Crime Scene Unit member and criminal investigator, I also processed serious-injury and fatal collision scenes. One severe vehicle-collision investigation resulted in a written commendation for professionalism. The standard remained the same in every case: follow the evidence, document it carefully, and treat everyone involved with dignity.

My vehicle-related work also extended beyond the roadside. I worked alongside Berla-trained examiners, assisted with removing vehicle infotainment systems, and personally analyzed the resulting vehicle data for investigative use. I supported search-warrant work involving vehicle electronic-module evidence. That does not make me a trained Berla examiner or an automotive damage appraiser, but it does show that I can learn unfamiliar vehicle systems, work carefully with technical evidence, and translate the result into a usable case record.

Customer service was never separate from the investigation. Drivers, victims, witnesses, families, students, and later real-estate clients often came to me during stressful situations with incomplete information and strong opinions about what should happen next. I learned to listen, ask direct questions, lower the temperature, explain what I could and could not do, and follow through. As a Field Training Officer for 18 years and a remote adjunct Criminal Justice faculty member for 18 years, I also learned how to teach complicated processes without talking down to people.

I am new to insurance policy interpretation, carrier claim systems, damage estimating, reserving, and settlement authority. Those are genuine gaps. The trainee structure is attractive because it provides the claims foundation while letting me contribute immediately in fact development, interviews, documentation, customer communication, fraud-potential recognition, organization, and sound judgment. This is a deliberate career transition, not a temporary stop while I look for another detective position.

National General's emphasis on investigating automobile losses and guiding customers through difficult situations is a strong match for the work I want to continue doing. I would welcome the chance to discuss how my collision-investigation background and willingness to learn the insurance side could serve your claims team.

Respectfully,

Troy Hokanson
