Dear Members of the Search Committee:

I am applying for the adjunct faculty position in Criminology and Criminal Justice at Metropolitan State University. I bring 25 years of law-enforcement and investigative experience, 8 years and 3 months of U.S. Army service, and 18 years of concurrent university-level adjunct teaching. I hold a Master of Arts in Police Leadership, Administration and Education and a Bachelor of Arts in Criminal Justice, magna cum laude. My background includes criminal investigations, digital and criminal forensics, crime-scene operations, field training, curriculum development, and adult education.

For 18 years with the University of Phoenix, I taught adult and nontraditional learners from varied professional, cultural, military, educational, and socioeconomic backgrounds. Many balanced coursework with employment, military obligations, family responsibilities, pregnancy, disabilities, and other significant life circumstances. I worked collaboratively with students receiving approved ADA accommodations and with students who disclosed pregnancy or other circumstances early in a course, helping them plan ahead, remain engaged, meet course expectations, and maximize their opportunity for success.

My commitment to student success is reflected in repeated recognition of my teaching. I was nominated for Faculty of the Year in 2020, received Faculty 500 recognition in 2020 and 2021, and received the John Sperling Award in 2024. These honors reflect a sustained commitment to student-centered instruction, meaningful engagement, timely feedback, academic rigor, and learning environments in which students can succeed despite varied personal, professional, and educational circumstances.

My teaching philosophy is grounded in fairness, accountability, accessibility, and respect. Equitable education does not mean lowering standards; it means removing unnecessary barriers, communicating expectations clearly, and giving students a genuine opportunity to demonstrate learning. My military service, campus public-safety work at St. Cloud State University, law-enforcement career, investigative assignments, and faculty experience have required me to work effectively with people from widely different backgrounds and perspectives. My own experience with a non-visible service-connected disability has also deepened my understanding that students may carry substantial challenges that are not immediately apparent.

I am particularly interested in criminal investigation, violent crime investigation, criminal and digital forensics, forensic evidence, crime-scene investigation, police communication, white-collar crime, crime analysis, investigative technology, police leadership and management, contemporary policing, and criminal-justice ethics. I would also welcome introductory or capstone assignments that connect theory, research, ethics, and practical decision-making.

Metro State's emphasis on relevant, student-centered education and equitable outcomes aligns closely with how I have taught and served throughout my career. I would be honored to contribute to the School of Criminology and Criminal Justice.

Sincerely,

Troy J. Hokanson
