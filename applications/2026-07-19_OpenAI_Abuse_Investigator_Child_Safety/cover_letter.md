That work required more than finding prohibited files. It required understanding the actor, the account, the device, the behavior, and the risk to a child. I used mobile and computer forensics, cloud-provider returns, location records, communications, OSINT, interviews, and timelines to connect those pieces. In one production case, immediate Google preservation and later analysis of synchronized cloud evidence recovered material the offender tried to delete. In another, mobile-device evidence supported a federal production conviction and a 15-year sentence. The point was always the same: identify the risk accurately, preserve what could disappear, communicate the findings clearly, and move the case to the people authorized to act.

I carried this specialized work alongside a full-time investigative caseload. An August 2021 case-management snapshot documents 236 unique Lakeville investigations assigned to me. I also rotated through week-long on-call assignments that generally recurred about every seven weeks. Those callouts and assigned cases included child abuse and neglect, vulnerable victims, serious violent crime, missing persons, fraud, harassment, burglary, and other high-risk matters. The work demanded rapid judgment, careful escalation, and reports that prosecutors, command staff, and partner agencies could understand without having been present for the investigation.
