I am a medically retired Minnesota detective with 25 years of sworn law enforcement service and an honorably discharged U.S. Army veteran with 8 years and 3 months of service. My title was detective rather than paralegal, but the substance of the work is familiar. I prepared and reviewed subpoenas, search warrants, preservation requests, affidavits, reports, evidence summaries, correspondence, and court exhibits. I used Minnesota court e-filing and court-record systems to submit, retrieve, verify, and track legal documents and case activity. I also worked directly with city and county prosecutors, law enforcement agencies, forensic specialists, service providers, victims, witnesses, and organizational stakeholders.

Digital evidence preservation and production were central to my electronic-crimes assignments. In 2020, I personally processed 5,304 GB of digital evidence while maintaining chain of custody, examination records, case notes, and clear reports for investigators and prosecutors. I identified relevant accounts, devices, records, and custodians; coordinated lawful collection; reviewed large evidence sets for investigative relevance; and translated technical findings for attorneys, courts, and juries.

I also understand what is different about this position. I have not worked as a corporate paralegal, conducted formal attorney-client privilege review, or used Relativity. I would not represent otherwise. I have, however, learned and worked across complex investigative, forensic, court, records, and workflow systems, including LexisNexis Accurint, X-Ways Forensics, Cellebrite UFED, Magnet AXIOM, Forensic Toolkit, Minnesota court systems, kvCORE, and KW Command. The platform gap is teachable. The judgment required to preserve evidence, manage sensitive requests, interview people fairly, document decisions, and meet legal deadlines was built over a career.

One investigation involved more than $360,000 in verified losses, three financial institutions, international fund movement, and coordination with Homeland Security Investigations and the Minnesota Department of Commerce Fraud Bureau. The matter resulted in a felony conviction and written commendation from an Assistant Dakota County Attorney. Work like that reinforced a basic lesson: legal process and document handling are not administrative details. They protect the integrity of the investigation and the people affected by it.

I am interested in the posted 20-hour-per-week position and would also welcome consideration for a full-time opportunity if Medtronic determines that my investigative and legal-process background fits a broader business need. Medtronic's mission is grounded in real human outcomes. I would value the chance to support that mission by helping the Government Investigations team respond carefully, lawfully, and on time.
