# Resume Review Decisions — 2026-08-29

## Bottom line

The review identifies several useful presentation changes, but its strongest claims should not all be treated as rules. The revised approach adopts a targeted headline, a tighter opening, stronger metric placement, and more selective treatment of older experience. It does not impose a one-page limit, delete relevant experience solely because it is more than 15 years old, or manufacture numbers for every role.

## Adopted

- Add a one-line, role-aligned headline below the contact row. A headline is positioning, not a claim that the target title is Troy's current job.
- Keep the professional summary short and scannable: two to four lines or, when useful, no more than three concise bullets.
- Put the most decision-useful verified metrics early when the sentence remains natural.
- Use specific, accurate verbs and remove obvious repetition.
- Give recent and role-relevant work the most space.
- Use standard ATS headings and a single-column, text-first structure for portal uploads.

## Modified rather than adopted literally

- **Length:** Two pages is appropriate for Troy's experience when both pages contain relevant evidence. One page is an option for narrowly targeted opportunities, not a universal requirement.
- **Older experience:** Relevant earlier work should be compressed into an `Earlier Public-Safety Experience` block rather than deleted by date alone.
- **Quantification:** Use verified, meaningful numbers. Do not force a number into every role or invent a proxy metric.
- **Numbers placement:** Lead selected proof points with numbers when that improves scanning; do not make every bullet read like a spreadsheet.
- **Summary format:** A short paragraph is acceptable. Bullets are optional, not mandatory.
- **Verb variation:** Prefer precise verbs over artificial synonym swapping.

## Claims not accepted as controlling guidance

- The review calls formatting weak while also awarding full scores for text extraction, single-column structure, standard text, layout, and font treatment. That contradiction points to a sales-oriented diagnosis rather than a demonstrated parsing failure.
- The statement that roughly 75% of qualified candidates are filtered out by ATS was not supported by a cited source in the review and is not used as a design rule.
