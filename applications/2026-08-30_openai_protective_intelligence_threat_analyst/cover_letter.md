Re: Protective Intelligence & Threat Analyst

OpenAI's Protective Intelligence & Threat Analyst role calls for judgment under uncertainty: finding the signal in public and restricted-source information, explaining what is known and what remains uncertain, and helping people act before risk becomes harm. That work closely matches the investigative habits I developed during 25 years of sworn public-safety service, including 6.5 years across two investigative rotations and 5.5 years in digital forensics.

As Lakeville Police Department's representative and digital-forensics resource in a ten-agency electronic-crimes task force, I independently managed examinations and investigative support while coordinating with investigators, supervisors, prosecutors, attorneys, service providers, and partner agencies. I developed and corroborated leads through public records, social media, LexisNexis Accurint, interviews, financial and provider records, and digital evidence. The work required disciplined source evaluation, clear handling of uncertainty, and concise reports for people who had to make consequential decisions without reviewing the underlying evidence themselves.

I also built repeatable methods. In Lakeville's Electronic Crimes Unit, I created an investigator resource covering preservation, subpoenas, search warrants, and service-provider requirements. Later, I led a multi-victim Business Email Compromise investigation that used financial tracing, shell-company research, and cross-case patterns to verify more than $360,000 in losses. The matter closed with a felony conviction. Across patrol and investigative assignments, I handled time-sensitive safety incidents, documented changing facts, and coordinated escalation and follow-up.

My background is adjacent to corporate protective intelligence, not a substitute for it. I have not run an executive-protection intelligence program, used Ontic, or completed ATAP training, and I would not present myself as a formal behavioral-threat specialist. What transfers directly is the ability to research people and networks, preserve and test evidence, distinguish fact from inference, communicate risk without exaggeration, and work across legal, technical, and operational boundaries. My current paid AI project work has also given me practical experience evaluating generated content and developing capability with source validation, GitHub, APIs, and Python basics.

OpenAI's security environment combines public visibility, fast-moving technology, and unusually high consequences for employees and operations. I would bring mature investigative judgment, careful writing, and a willingness to learn the protective-intelligence disciplines where my experience is not yet direct. I would welcome a candid conversation about that transfer and the role's operational needs.
