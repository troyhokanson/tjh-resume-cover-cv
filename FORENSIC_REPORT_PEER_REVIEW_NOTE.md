## Documented Practice

- The Electronic Crimes Task Force laboratory used mandatory peer review for forensic examination reports.
- Every forensic report Troy wrote was reviewed by another qualified examiner.
- The reviewing examiner provided feedback before the report was finalized or released.
- This was a routine quality-control requirement, not an occasional or informal courtesy review.

## Strategic Value

This experience demonstrates:

- Forensic-report quality assurance
- Independent technical review
- Openness to examiner feedback and revision
- Clear, supportable, and reproducible reporting
- Awareness of how findings must withstand investigative, prosecutorial, courtroom, and peer scrutiny
- Collaborative laboratory practice
- Professional accountability for conclusions, limitations, and wording

## Interview Positioning

A strong interview explanation is:

> Every forensic report I wrote was reviewed by another examiner, and I received feedback before the report was finalized. That process reinforced accuracy, clarity, technical support, and professional humility. It also meant I had to write findings so another qualified examiner could understand the workflow, evaluate the conclusions, and identify anything that needed clarification or correction.
