## 2. Forensic Platform Progression

### AccessData FTK and FTK Imager — 2016–2018

- FTK was the primary forensic processing and examination platform early in Troy's Electronic Crimes Task Force work.
- FTK Imager supported acquisition, preview, and imaging workflows.
- As Troy became more proficient with X-Ways Forensics and later used Magnet AXIOM for artifact-centered analysis, FTK was relegated to a backup role.
- Accurate positioning: `Primary early-career task-force platform; later retained as a backup after transitioning to X-Ways and AXIOM.`

### X-Ways Forensics — 2018–2021

- X-Ways became Troy's principal computer-forensics examination tool.
- It reflects his mature computer-forensics workflow and should generally lead when a role emphasizes computer examination, examiner judgment, technical efficiency, evidence integrity, or tool-selection expertise.
- Accurate positioning: `Principal computer-forensics examination platform from 2018 through 2021.`

### Magnet AXIOM — 2019–2021

- Used selectively for artifact-based examination and presentation.
- Particularly useful for iPhones, application and messaging artifacts, and some Mac laptop examinations.
- AXIOM became increasingly important toward the end of Troy's investigative rotation but did not replace X-Ways as the principal general computer-forensics platform.
- Accurate positioning: `Selective and increasingly important artifact-centered platform used from 2019 through 2021 for iPhones, application data, messaging artifacts, and some Mac examinations.`

### Canonical progression statement

> Used AccessData FTK as the primary forensic platform from 2016 through 2018, then transitioned to X-Ways Forensics as the principal computer-forensics examination tool from 2018 through 2021. Magnet AXIOM was used selectively from 2019 through 2021 for artifact-centered analysis, iPhones, application and messaging data, and some Mac laptop examinations, while FTK remained available as a backup.

## 3. Primary Mobile-Forensics Workflow — 2016–2021

### Cellebrite Touch 2

- Located at the Dakota County Electronic Crimes Task Force.
- Used as a primary mobile-device extraction platform during Troy's 2016–2021 task-force period.

### Cellebrite UFED 4PC

- Dongle-based UFED 4PC access was available both at the Electronic Crimes Task Force and at Lakeville Police Department.
- This allowed Troy to work within the Cellebrite workflow at the task-force laboratory and from his home agency.

### Cellebrite Physical Analyzer

- Used for decoding, review, analysis, and reporting of mobile-device evidence.
- Part of the primary mobile-forensics workflow throughout the 2016–2021 investigative period.

### GrayKey — approximately 2019–2021

- Became the primary iOS device-access tool beginning around 2019.
- Worked in conjunction with the broader Cellebrite-centered mobile-forensics workflow.

### Canonical mobile statement

> From 2016 through 2021, used Cellebrite Touch 2, UFED 4PC, and Physical Analyzer as the primary mobile-forensics workflow, with UFED 4PC available at both the Electronic Crimes Task Force and Lakeville Police Department. GrayKey became the primary iOS access tool beginning around 2019.

## 6. Tableau Write Blockers and Validation Practice

- Troy used Tableau built-in and portable hardware write blockers in forensic acquisition workflows.
- He recalls a model number beginning with `T3` and a sequence similar to `Ti345678`, but the exact model is not verified.
- The unit was new around 2018.
- **All mounted and portable write blockers were tested and validated at least annually to confirm that they were functioning properly and continued to prevent writes to source media.**
- This was an evidence-integrity and quality-assurance practice, not merely equipment familiarity.

### Strategic value

The annual validation practice demonstrates:

- Forensic-laboratory quality control
- Evidence-integrity discipline
- Awareness that hardware write protection must be verified rather than assumed
- Repeatable acquisition procedures
- Defensible chain-of-custody and examination practices
- Readiness for audit, testimony, peer review, or procedural scrutiny

### Permitted wording

- `Used Tableau built-in and portable hardware write blockers in forensic acquisition workflows and participated in at least annual testing and validation to verify proper write-protection performance.`
- `Maintained defensible acquisition practices by ensuring mounted and portable write blockers were tested and validated at least annually.`

### Verification rule

Until documentation or photographs confirm the model, do not publish a specific Tableau model number.

Do not overstate the role as laboratory accreditation management, formal calibration authority, or sole ownership of the validation program unless additional records support those claims.

## 8. Genetec AutoVu ALPR Project — 2007

### Project overview

- Approximately $40,000 ALPR project funded through a Target Corporation grant.
- Used Genetec AutoVu technology.
- Troy served as the agency-side operational coordinator and the project's practical eyes and ears.

### Troy's documented responsibilities

- Coordinated with Target Corporation regarding the corporate grant and project use case.
- Worked with Lakeville Police Department leadership to define and support the operational purpose.
- Coordinated with city or department IT.
- Coordinated with the Minnesota Bureau of Criminal Apprehension and CJIS requirements.
- Helped assemble the operational use case and agency workflow.
- Helped arrange completion of a Wi-Fi connection in the underground garage.
- The secure connection allowed the BCA to upload an updated records file to the squad vehicle every midnight.
- Helped troubleshoot connectivity and camera issues with Genetec when problems arose.
- Provided the agency-side operational perspective throughout implementation and use.

### Canonical positioning statement

> In 2007, served as the agency-side operational coordinator for a roughly $40,000 Target Corporation-funded Genetec AutoVu ALPR project. Coordinated the use case with Target, Lakeville leadership, IT, and BCA CJIS; helped establish the secure underground-garage Wi-Fi workflow that allowed BCA to upload updated records to the squad each midnight; and worked with Genetec to troubleshoot connectivity and camera issues. Functioned as the project's practical eyes and ears from the end-user side.
