## 1. Strategic Direction

Troy's primary public-safety technology strategy is to pursue **senior individual-contributor roles with companies serving state, local, and education (SLED) public-safety customers, especially law-enforcement agencies**.

The preferred lane is not generic technology sales and is not limited to investigator job titles. The strongest fit is where an employer needs someone who can:

- Understand law-enforcement operations from patrol through major investigations.
- Translate technical products into practical agency workflows.
- Train sworn officers, analysts, investigators, supervisors, command staff, prosecutors, and other non-technical audiences.
- Guide onboarding, implementation, adoption, customer success, professional services, or pre-sales discovery.
- Bring credible end-user experience with investigative, digital-forensic, evidence, camera, ALPR, and intelligence platforms.
- Communicate calmly and credibly without relying on command-presence theatrics or aggressive sales behavior.

### Primary career thesis

> Troy is a former end user, investigator, trainer, adjunct faculty member, and operational subject-matter expert who can help public-safety agencies understand, adopt, and obtain measurable value from mission-critical technology.

### Default role level

Prioritize **senior individual-contributor and senior practitioner roles**. A title containing `Manager` does not automatically mean direct people management. Evaluate the actual duties.

Remain open to selective lead or people-manager opportunities when the position is primarily based on subject-matter leadership, customer outcomes, program ownership, training, implementation, or investigative expertise.

## 2. Remote-First Rules

Remote work is a core strategy requirement because Troy plans to relocate from Minnesota to southwest Washington and wants career continuity through the move.

### Location priority

1. **Fully remote, United States**
2. **Home-based or territory-based with reasonable travel**
3. **Remote with a geographic preference that includes Minnesota, Washington, Oregon, Pacific Northwest, Midwest, West, or nationwide**
4. **Hybrid near Minneapolis before relocation**, only when the opportunity is unusually strong
5. **Hybrid near Portland, Vancouver, Ridgefield, or southwest Washington after relocation**

### Travel priority

- **0-25% travel:** preferred
- **26-40% travel:** acceptable for a strong role
- **41-50% travel:** selective; weigh health, recovery, and quality of life
- **More than 50% travel:** apply only when compensation, role fit, and long-term value justify it
- A role labeled `remote` may still require substantial field travel. Always read the travel requirement.

### Remote-validation rule

Remote posture changes. Never assume a company is permanently remote-friendly based on an older posting. Verify the live job description before scoring or applying.

Use these labels:

- **R1 — Confirmed remote-compatible:** current official evidence of remote or home-based U.S. roles in relevant functions
- **R2 — Frequently remote, field-based, or territory-based:** strong target, but validate each posting
- **R3 — Mixed or location-dependent:** apply when the individual role meets location requirements

## 7. Search Logic for Automated Job Scans

A job scanner should mark a position as a likely public-safety vendor match when:

1. The employer sells technology, analytics, training, hardware, software, or professional services to law enforcement, public safety, courts, corrections, prosecutors, emergency communications, or campus public safety; and
2. The title or body includes one or more target role-family signals; and
3. The role is remote, home-based, territory-based, or geographically compatible; and
4. The required experience can be met through Troy's operational, investigative, instructional, customer-facing, or technical background without overclaiming.

### Negative or caution signals

Reduce the fit score when a posting is primarily:

- Quota-heavy new-logo sales with little customer, technical, or public-safety content
- Software engineering or coding

- Direct people management requiring a long record of formal employee supervision
- Heavy aviation operations requiring extensive logged flight hours Troy does not have
- More than 50% travel without a compelling strategic reason
- Onsite-only outside Minnesota before the move or outside southwest Washington / Portland after the move
- Commission-only or contractor-only
- Generic customer support with no meaningful public-safety, investigative, training, or technical ownership

## 8. Application Positioning Rules

### Use these themes

- Former end user of the technology category
- Investigator who understands what agencies need when a case is moving quickly
- Teacher who can make technical concepts usable
- Customer advocate grounded in agency reality
- Senior individual contributor capable of owning outcomes without needing a command title
- Cross-functional bridge among users, command staff, IT, legal, product, engineering, sales, and customer success
- Calm, credible, practical, and accountable

### Do not use these themes

- Generic `retired cop looking for a second career`
- Overreliance on rank or command presence
- `Born salesperson` framing
- DFR expert without direct operational experience
- Product expertise that exceeds documented hands-on use
- People-manager claims based only on informal leadership
- Tactical experience as the primary value proposition unless the product directly supports tactical operations

## 10. Standing Career Decision

Public-safety vendor roles are a **primary career lane**, not a side experiment.

When a strong remote or home-based role appears in customer success, solutions consulting, professional services, implementation, training, technical account management, public-safety strategy, investigative analytics, digital evidence, DFIR, ALPR, RTCC, body-worn camera, CAD/RMS, or DFR, the system should evaluate it seriously even when the title does not contain `investigator`.

This lane may take priority over unrelated certification study or lower-fit investigative positions when the role provides:

- Sustainable remote work
- Strong compensation and benefits
- Meaningful use of Troy's law-enforcement and teaching background
- A senior individual-contributor path
- Reasonable travel
- Career continuity through the planned move to Washington
- Work that supports investigators, officers, analysts, and agencies without requiring Troy to return to frontline law-enforcement stress
